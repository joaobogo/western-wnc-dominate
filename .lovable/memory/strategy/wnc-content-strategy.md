# Western North Carolina Content Strategy

## Strategic Goal
Make Highlander's site the most locally authoritative roofing and construction resource in WNC — not through volume but through specificity, genuine expertise, and useful local detail that no national competitor can replicate.

## Content Themes

### 1. Mountain Weather Impacts on Roofs & Homes
- Freeze-thaw cycling at elevation (3,000-4,500 ft)
- UV intensity at altitude — accelerated material degradation
- 60-80+ inches annual rainfall (Cashiers/Highlands plateau)
- Snow loads and ice dam formation
- Sustained ridge-top wind exposure
- Summer thunderstorm damage patterns
- Microclimate variation by elevation and slope orientation

### 2. Regional Storm Preparation
- Pre-winter roof inspection checklists (by elevation zone)
- Hurricane remnant wind events (late summer/fall)
- Spring severe thunderstorm prep
- Ice storm response and prevention
- Tree-fall risk assessment for mountain lots

### 3. Seasonal Maintenance for WNC
- **Spring:** Post-winter damage assessment, moss/algae treatment, gutter clearing
- **Summer:** UV protection, ventilation checks, storm damage monitoring
- **Fall:** Pre-winter prep, leaf/debris clearing, heat cable installation
- **Winter:** Ice dam monitoring, emergency tarp response, snow load assessment

### 4. Local Architecture Considerations
- Mountain timber-frame and log home roofing challenges
- Steep-pitch roofing (10/12 to 14/12) — material and labor implications
- Multi-gable and complex roof geometries common in WNC estates
- Cedar shake longevity at elevation vs. lowland
- Metal roofing performance in mountain climates
- Vacation rental vs. primary residence roofing needs
- Historic home sensitivity in towns like Highlands, Brevard, Saluda

### 5. Local Homeowner Concerns
- Finding reliable contractors in rural mountain areas
- Storm chaser avoidance after weather events
- Insurance claims for mountain-specific damage
- Cost differences: mountain vs. lowland NC
- Seasonal scheduling constraints (winter access, short building windows)
- Property manager coordination for second homes/rentals

### 6. Town & County Specific Content
- **By Elevation Zone:**
  - High (4,000+ ft): Highlands, Cashiers — extreme UV, ice, rainfall
  - Mid (2,500-4,000 ft): Sylva, Waynesville, Brevard — balanced challenges
  - Valley (2,000-2,500 ft): Franklin, Hendersonville — standard mountain weather
- **By County:** Macon, Jackson, Haywood, Transylvania, Henderson, Buncombe, Swain, Cherokee
- Content connects to existing town pages for SEO internal linking

## Reusable Content Modules

### WNCClimateCallout
- Inline card with mountain icon + elevation/weather fact
- Use in: any article, service page, project detail
- Example: "At 4,100 ft, Highlands receives 80+ inches of rain annually — demanding roofing systems most lowland contractors never spec for."

### ElevationZoneCard
- Shows elevation range, weather profile, recommended materials
- Use in: material guides, town pages, service pages
- Variants: compact (sidebar), full (standalone section)

### SeasonalTimeline
- Horizontal timeline showing WNC roofing/construction seasons
- Marks: winter shutdown, spring prep window, peak build season, fall close-out
- Use in: project planning articles, homepage, about page

### LocalProofBadge
- Small badge: "Serving [Town] Since 2017" or "[X] Projects in [County]"
- Use in: town pages, local articles, sidebar, form pages

### MountainWeatherFact
- Single-stat card: icon + number + context line
- Example: "80+ inches — annual rainfall in Cashiers, NC"
- Use in: throughout site as trust/authority micro-signals

### CountyServiceMap
- Interactive or static map showing 8-county service area
- Clickable towns linking to town pages
- Use in: service areas page, about page, footer

## Internal Linking Architecture

```
Town Pages ←→ Town-Tagged Blog Articles
     ↕                    ↕
Service Pages ←→ Category Blog Articles
     ↕                    ↕
Division Hubs ←→ Project Detail Pages
```

### Linking Rules:
1. Every town-tagged article links to its town page
2. Every town page links to 2-3 relevant blog articles
3. Every service page links to 1-2 educational articles
4. Every article links to 1 service page and 1 town page minimum
5. Project detail pages link to the relevant service page + town page

## Homepage Integration Points

1. **BuiltForWNC section** — already built, shows 6 climate factors
2. **TownGrid** — already built, links to town pages
3. **BlogInsights** — feature WNC-tagged articles in rotation
4. **ProofStrip** — "8 counties served" stat reinforces local authority
5. **FeaturedProjects** — show location/elevation on project cards

## Service Page Integration

Each service page should include:
1. WNCClimateCallout relevant to that service (e.g., "ice dams" on repair page)
2. Town-specific examples in content copy
3. Link to 1-2 local blog articles
4. ElevationZoneCard in sidebar for material recommendations

## Recurring Article Series Ideas

1. **"Roofing at Elevation"** — Monthly deep dives on mountain-specific challenges
2. **"Town Spotlight"** — Quarterly features on specific WNC towns + roofing needs
3. **"Storm Season Update"** — As-needed weather event coverage
4. **"Mountain Material Report"** — Seasonal material performance observations
5. **"Ask Highlander"** — Q&A format answering real homeowner questions
